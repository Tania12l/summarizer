### A Simple Regularization of the Smooth Quantum Hydrodynamic Model

**Authors:** Carl L. Gardner

- Introduces a regularization replacing density gradients in the momentum equation with a Boltzmann distribution to suppress unstable modes.
- Applies the regularized smooth quantum hydrodynamic model to a resonant tunneling diode, reproducing realistic negative differential resistance and hysteresis.
- Demonstrates close agreement with fully quantum mechanical simulations for the diode’s current‑voltage characteristics.

### Heat Transfer and Torque in Enclosing Cylindrical Configurations with Nonreciprocal Materials

**Authors:** Dhruv Shah, Kiryl Asheichyk, David Gelbwaser-Klimovsky, Noah Graham, Mehran Kardar, Matthias Kr\"uger

- Torque in enclosing cylindrical setups is bounded by heat transfer when at least one object is rotationally symmetric; nonreciprocal media are required for nonzero torque if both are symmetric.
- For concentric cylinders with nonreciprocal dielectric, heat transfer and torque are expressed via an angular‑momentum‑resolved flux Φₙ(ω), and a new analytic Casimir force formula for dilute plates at different temperatures is derived.
- A bound between torque, friction, and heat transfer limits the efficiency of a contactless rotational heat engine.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24967)

### Nonlocal thermal noise in electrically coupled conductors: A microscopic two-dimensional study

**Authors:** Jorge Berger

- Simulations of capacitively coupled wires show temperature‑gradient‑induced nonlocal correlations in electromotive forces, breaking the local‑noise assumption.
- The model reproduces Ohm’s law, equilibrium Johnson noise, and zero correlations at equal temperature, but predicts systematic deviations in inferred Johnson temperature when wires differ in temperature.
- Results suggest that independent local thermal‑noise sources are invalid for electrically coupled conductors out of equilibrium, even with short‑range interactions.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24980)

### Photonic spin-Hall effect as a probe for time-reversal-symmetry broken band topological phases

**Authors:** Deblina Samanta, Darshan G. Joshi

- PSHE causes spatial splitting of reflected Gaussian beam into opposite circular polarizations.
- Centroid shift of the split beams directly measures optical Hall conductivity, revealing topological order.
- Frequency‑dependent sign of the shift distinguishes time‑reversal‑symmetry‑broken topological phases from trivial ones.
