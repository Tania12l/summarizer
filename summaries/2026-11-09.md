### Transformer-Based Characterization of Criticality in Directed Percolation

**Authors:** Bozheng Dou, Wei Li

- Transformer encoder classifies active vs absorbing phases in (1+D and (2+D bond directed percolation, accurately recovering critical points and exponents.
- Attention rollout defines a temporal attention center that reorganizes near criticality, revealing finite‑size scaling behavior.
- The model transfers between fully occupied and single‑seed initial states, showing shared critical information with directional transfer asymmetry.

### Permutation order governs basin entropy in critical one-input Boolean networks

**Authors:** Ruodan Liu, Ahana Ghosh, Sean Campbell, Venkata Sai Narayana Bavisetty, Matthew Wheeler, Claus Kadelka

- Basin entropy is determined by the permutation order of feedback loops in critical one‑input Boolean networks.
- Independent loop oscillations produce phase offsets that cause linear‑in‑m deviations from additive entropy predictions.
- Ensemble‑averaged entropy remains additive to first subleading order, with discrepancies appearing only at \(O(\ln m\,\ln\ln m)\).

### Devitrification: crystal nucleation and growth mechanisms in glasses

**Authors:** Max Brown, Peter Wolynes

- RFOT theory predicts devitrification via ordinary nucleation, accelerated by the glass’s frozen-in energy.
- At low temperatures, nanonucleation produces nanometer‑scale crystals, driven by local stability fluctuations.
- Rare, less‑stable percolating regions trigger a dramatic speedup of nucleation and growth, explaining anomalous crystal growth in deeply supercooled systems.

### Lefschetz-Thimble Method to the Sign Problem in the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev Model

**Authors:** Yin-Hao Fang, Yang Qi

- Applied Lefschetz‑thimble and complex Langevin techniques to the SYK model, reformulating its partition function as a high‑dimensional complex integral.
- Demonstrated numerical agreement with high‑temperature expansions, infrared solutions, and DQMC, validating the framework.
- Showed that the complex Langevin search identifies the dominant saddle points in the large‑N limit, offering a non‑perturbative solution to the SYK sign problem.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09168)

### Coexistence of antiferromagnetism and topological $s+d+id$ superconductivity in twisted cuprate bilayers

**Authors:** Javier Castro Luaces, Manuel Fern\'andez L\'opez, Jorge Bravo-Abad, Jaime Merino

- Twist ≈ 45° in cuprate bilayers induces topological s + d + id superconductivity that coexists with antiferromagnetism at low hole doping.
- The phase exhibits a 2‑fold rotational symmetry in ARPES, unlike competing d + id or s + id states.
- A finite Josephson current at 45° twist and Majorana edge modes seen in STM unambiguously identify the topological s + d + id state.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09469)

### Pressure-induced symmetry breaking and polarity in the van der Waals magnet CrSBr

**Authors:** Amit Pawbake, Sitaram Ramakrishnan, Kseniia Mosina, Aljoscha Soll, Zdenek Sofer, Pierre Rodiere, Rolf Heid, Gaston Garbarino, Pierre Bouvier, Clement Faugeras

- Pressure drives CrSBr from orthorhombic Pmmn to monoclinic P2₁/m (~5 GPa) and then to polar orthorhombic Pmm2 (~5 GPa).

- Hydrostatic pressure thus couples crystal symmetry, polarity, and electronic properties in this van der Waals magnet.
