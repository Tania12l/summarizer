### Correlated Low-Energy Model of Monolayer 1H-NbS$_2$: A cRPA+DMFT Study

**Authors:** Karollaine C. Leite, Jorge L. B. de Faria, Luiz Antonio Ribeiro Junior, Alexandre C. Dias, Helena Bragan\c{c}a, Alberto S. de Arruda

- Single‑band Nb‑\(d_{z^2}\) model reproduces ARPES/STS features, including the Van Hove singularity.
- cRPA gives \(U_{\text{cRPA}}=138\) eV; DFT+DMFT captures strong correlations and spectral weight redistribution, outperforming static DFT+U.
- The minimal model reproduces spectral broadening seen in more elaborate multi‑orbital and electron‑phonon treatments, validating the downfolded approach.

### Disorder into Correlation: A Microscopic Mechanism for Sub-McIntyre Avalanche Noise

**Authors:** Shafat Shahnewaz, Hannaneh Karimi, Joe C. Campbell, Avik W. Ghosh

- Polar‑optical‑phonon emission confined to a forward cone competes with alloy scattering, limiting carrier cooling.
- Reduced phonon cooling narrows the ionization path beyond dead space, synchronizing ionizations into a dynamic staircase.
- Explains sub‑McIntyre avalanche noise in Sb alloys and HgCdTe, making alloy disorder a tunable design parameter.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06915)

### Time-Reversal Selection Rule for Twist Disorder

**Authors:** Peng Kang, Da Wan, Shulin Bai, Pengfei Zhang, Vincent Michaud-Rioux, Zhen Li, Yu Liu, Lei Zheng, Li-Dong Zhao, Huibin Xu

- Time‑reversal selection rule: twist harmonics that are frame rotations cancel backscattering; those that change coupling scatter.
- Zero‑set theorem gives universal thickness laws for electrons/phonons, set by band‑edge symmetry; random twist rescale but keep the same scaling order.
- Unprotected axes yield a transparent energy with heavy‑tailed disorder and an \(N^{-3}\) conductance decay; black‑phosphorus films demonstrate symmetry filtering, keeping ~100× more electron than hole conductance at 64 layers.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06943)

### Interplay between spinodal decomposition and wetting in ternary to quinary mixtures

**Authors:** Sandeep Kumar, Supriyo Ghosh

- 2D Cahn–Hilliard simulations of N = 3–5 component mixtures show surface‑directed spinodal decomposition around a filler particle.
- Wetting versus non‑wetting interfacial tensions generate lattice, droplet, and target (core‑shell) morphologies that later coarsen via curvature‑driven depletion.
- Coarsening slows with more components, but wetting accelerates it, illustrating how particle–component interactions control microstructural evolution.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06946)

### $\mathcal{PT}$-symmetry as Effective Time Reversal Symmetry for Anderson Localization in a Collinear Antiferromagnet

**Authors:** Kim-Khuong Huynh, Takuma Ogasawara, Motoi Kimata, Sofie S{\o}by Leiszner, Nhu-Quynh Thi Phan, Michael Anthony Quintero, Frej S{\o}ren Rattenborg, Denis Ar\v{c}on, Bo Brummerstedt Iversen, Katsumi Tanigaki

- PT symmetry acts as the effective time‑reversal symmetry for Anderson localization in BaMn₂Bi₂, even though ordinary TR symmetry is broken by antiferromagnetic order.
- Magnetoconductance follows standard TR‑breaking laws, but the degree of symmetry breaking is modulated by magnetocrystalline anisotropy that stabilizes PT‑related sublattices.
- Transverse magnetic fields strongly suppress localization by canting PT sublattices, whereas longitudinal fields have only a minor effect, highlighting the robustness governed by exchange and magnetocrystalline interactions.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06951)

### CrystalJev: thinking fast and slow with atomistic foundation models for materials discovery

**Authors:** Peng Kang, Zhen Li, Yu Liu, Lei Zheng, Huibin Xu

- CrystalJev performs a single frozen‑potential query per unrelaxed structure, outputting calibrated probabilities and finite‑sample guarantees instead of full relaxations.
- Across 65 Matbench Discovery models, a “stable” call is a probability derived from model errors and candidate population, matching relaxation accuracy at ~1/30th the cost.
- A value‑of‑information rule sends expensive relaxations only when predictions can change, and prospective tests show single‑pass forecasts overestimate stability by ≤1 % on unseen candidates.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06985)

### Identifying Plastic Inorganic Semiconductors Requires More Rigorous Criteria

**Authors:** Qiao Wang

- GeSe tolerates ~23 % strain under macroscopic compression perpendicular to layers, yet fractures brittlely when bent along the c‑axis armchair direction.
- Conventional micropillar compression causes brittle fragmentation, whereas eccentric loading introduces shear that activates interlayer sliding and accommodates deformation.
- The study demonstrates that single‑direction or single‑scale tests are insufficient; a systematic, multi‑direction, multi‑scale evaluation framework is required to reliably identify plastic inorganic semiconductors.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07007)

### A numerical and efficient model for thermo-field electron emission calculations

**Authors:** Salvador Barranco C\'arceles, Andreas Kyritsakis, Anthony Ayari

- Introduces GETELEC‑3, a fast open‑source solver for the 1D time‑independent Schrödinger equation that replaces Fowler‑Nordheim/Murphy‑Good approximations.
- Employs the Numerov algorithm (≤1 % uncertainty) and a parallel kernel to compute current density in milliseconds; a neural‑network residual model achieves <4 % error at even lower cost.
- Supports arbitrary potentials and density of states for metals and semiconductors, with a user‑friendly interface and compiled executable.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07013)

### Spontaneous Motion Generates Reversible Nonreciprocity in an Achiral Active Elastic Ring

**Authors:** Wen-de Tian, Kang Chen, Tianhui Zhang

- Spontaneous rotation of an achiral active elastic ring creates mechanical nonreciprocity through off‑center elastic forces that couple deformation to propulsion.
- Reversing the rotation flips the antisymmetric response; a control scheme can preserve directionality while suppressing the near‑Hopf resonance.
- Near the Hopf threshold, the nonreciprocal response diverges inversely with distance to onset, giving rise to a self‑sustained traveling deformation above threshold.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07050)

### Direct-Write Chemical Vapor Deposition

**Authors:** Eeshan Ketkar, Swarnabha Chattaraj, Koichi Tanaka, Samy Kouidri, Supratik Guha

- 10 µm glass micro‑nozzle enables direct‑write CVD, producing 500 nm Al lines with 30 µm linewidth on TiN/Si in UHV.
- Continuous single‑pass, maskless growth allows systematic variation of deposition conditions on a single substrate.
- Transport model couples Knudsen emission and surface diffusion to quantify precursor diffusion length (L_d), which shrinks from 4 µm to 2 µm as temperature increases.

### Heavy-fermion hybridization waves from Dirac spinons near a Kondo-breakdown quantum phase transition

**Authors:** Pedro M. C\^onsoli, Matthias Vojta, Onur Erten

- Hybridization waves emerge in frustrated Kondo lattices near a Kondo‑breakdown quantum phase transition, where local moments form a Dirac spin liquid.
- The wave vector \(\vec{Q}\) connects Dirac spinon nodes to low‑energy electronic states, setting the spatial periodicity.
- The wave amplitude is governed by a kinematic condition between electronic and spinon band structures, explaining observed spatially modulated screening.

### Local-moment magnets on all close-packed lattices are equivalent

**Authors:** Sam Studdy, Alwyn Jose Raja, R. Ganesh

- Hidden symmetry links classical Heisenberg, XY, and Ising magnets on all close‑packed lattices when only nearest‑ and next‑nearest‑neighbour couplings (J₁, J₂) are present.
- The symmetry guarantees identical ground‑state energies and projected momenta for every close‑packed lattice, yielding a unified phase diagram.
- Fluctuations break this symmetry, so order‑by‑disorder effects can differ among close‑packed structures.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07158)

### Exchange self-energy and vertex corrections to static screening in the two-dimensional electron gas

**Authors:** Sankar Das Sarma

- Analytically derived leading beyond‑ring corrections (exchange self‑energy and vertex) to the static polarizability of a 2D electron gas.
- Individual divergent terms cancel, yielding a finite result that reproduces the Hartree‑Fock compressibility and renormalizes the Thomas‑Fermi wave vector.
- Exchange converts the one‑sided 2D Kohn kink into a sharp two‑sided peak, boosting Friedel oscillations 2–3× over RPA and affecting Kohn‑Luttinger pairing.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08309)
