### Diagonal Pair Coulomb Density Matrix on a Sphere: a numerical study

**Authors:** Riccardo Fantoni

- Tabulated diagonal $n$th powers ($n\le5$) of the primitive pair‑Coulomb density matrix on a sphere.
- Examined contact behavior as $n$ increases, revealing quantum effects absent classically.
- Found that quantum statistics obviates the need for artificial regularization (e.g., hard core) required in classical Coulomb systems.

### Catastrophes, Optical Multistabilities, and Chiral Photocurrent Hysteresis in Driven Weyl Semimetals

**Authors:** Christopher Yang, Gil Refael, Frederik Nathan

- Nonlinear plasma screening in Weyl semimetals amplifies photoresponse, producing bistability, hysteresis, and catastrophes.
- Bistability originates from a linear‑to‑saturated polarization crossover limited by the bounded Fermi velocity of Weyl carriers.
- Time‑reversal symmetry breaking and tilted nodes add a Berry‑curvature term, yielding helicity‑selective in‑plane currents in the nonlinear response.

### Staying positive: bounds for non-Gaussian noise in Schwinger-Keldysh effective field theory

**Authors:** Andrea Amoretti, Daniel K. Brattan

- SK EFTs for dissipative hydrodynamics contain non‑Gaussian thermal noise; symmetry constraints alone do not guarantee a valid probability distribution.
- Sharp, optimal bounds on cumulants are derived, ensuring positivity and realized by explicit positive distributions for both negative and positive kurtosis.
- Closed‑form nonlinear response, an exact sampling algorithm, and applications to microscopic and holographic models set a lower bound on the time step for a quartic Brownian model and pass a continuum fourth‑order positivity test.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12050)

### Real-space Visualization of Emergent Electron Crystals in Rhombohedral Graphene

**Authors:** Yiming Sun, Jinghao Deng, Jiabin Xie, Donghan Ge, Hongyuan Li, Takashi Taniguchi, Kenji Watanabe, Xiaomeng Liu

- STM/STS imaging of rhombohedral hexalayer graphene shows hole‑doping–induced electron crystals that switch from honeycomb to oblique order via a first‑order quantum phase transition.
- Quasiparticle‑interference and transport data reveal a Fermi surface inconsistent with nesting, indicating a metallic electron crystal where only a subset of carriers crystallize.
- The honeycomb crystal overlaps with multiferroic orbital magnetism, is stabilized by weak magnetic fields, suggesting a possible metallic anomalous Hall crystal.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12057)

### Spin splitting without symmetry: a nearly compensated ferrimagnet and the origin of altermagnetism

**Authors:** Joo Yull Rhee

- Spin splitting in altermagnets arises from anisotropic magnetic orbital–ligand arrangements, not from crystal symmetry.
- A triclinic Mn oxide lacking sublattice symmetry shows full Brillouin‑zone splitting; restoring inversion symmetry reduces it by ~100×.
- Symmetry can only reverse or forbid an existing splitting; it cannot create one, so the arrangement dictates the splitting across all crystal classes.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12931)

### Orbital Hall effect and orbital altermagnetism in even- and odd-parity-wave magnetic Lieb lattices

**Authors:** B\"orge G\"obel, Ersoy \c{S}a\c{s}{\i}o\u{g}lu, Samir Lounis

- Collinear AFM Lieb lattice produces d‑wave altermagnetism and an orbital Hall effect without SOC, resembling a ferromagnet’s transport.
- SOC adds a spin Hall response and orbital altermagnetism; anomalous Hall appears only with SOC and symmetry constraints.
- Odd‑parity p‑wave magnetism yields anisotropic orbital Hall effect and a symmetric conductivity tensor.
