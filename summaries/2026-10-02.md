### Transferable Graph Neural Network Surrogates for Molecular Dynamics Across Crystal Symmetries

**Authors:** Judah Immanuel, Avik Mahata, Aniruddha Maiti

- Single GNN surrogate predicts atomic displacements for MD across FCC Al, BCC Fe, HCP Mg without forces or integration.
- Achieves sub‑Ångström accuracy (~10⁻⁴ Å²) and stable nanosecond‑scale autoregressive propagation.
- Reproduces thermodynamic and structural features (RDF, MSD, coordination shells) without symmetry‑specific tuning.

### Emergence of electronic modes and triplet pairing from spin-1 antiferromagnetic insulators in the Kanamori-Hubbard model

**Authors:** Masanori Kohno

- Doping and temperature generate new electronic modes inside the gap of spin‑1 antiferromagnetic insulators in the two‑orbital Kanamori‑Hubbard model.
- These modes exhibit momentum‑shifted spin‑mode dispersions; doping‑induced modes differ between orbitals, whereas temperature‑induced modes follow the low‑energy spin mode.
- With weak inter‑orbital repulsion, doping induces onsite spin‑triplet pairing that opens a pair‑breaking gap, while temperature‑induced modes persist.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00110)

### Loading history and window geometry bound compact-state slip ranking during granular shear startup

**Authors:** Ruixin Zhou, Boliang Yu

- Compact neural score of stress, pressure, coordination, non‑affine motion, and force‑network metrics ranks near‑slip windows better than prevalence but weaker than loading‑history (elapsed strain) alone.
- Recovery‑aware analysis keeps 4 % of activity‑gated events, favoring longer preceding intervals, yet remains compatible with a geometry‑null model.
- No distinct short‑horizon precursor emerges beyond loading history; compact observables provide temporally aligned slip information but are bounded by loading‑history and window‑geometry effects.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00124)

### Quasi-static loading of granular media as a linear complementarity problem

### Migration Flows: Population Scaling and Heavy Tails

**Authors:** Cook Hyun Kim, Sangjoon Park, Seoyoung Jin, Jaiyong Lee

- Migration between counties explains population scaling of mean intercity flows and heavy‑tailed net‑flow distributions.
- Density‑dependent departure/arrival activities make mean flows scale with origin/destination populations; county‑pair net flows are Gaussian with SD ∝ mean.
- Averaging over a correlation distribution yields an intermediate power law with a Gaussian cutoff, and a stochastic model generates the required variance distribution.

### Causality violations in cluster GW+DMFT: Exact Lehmann moments and the necessity of non-local vertex corrections

**Authors:** Michael O. Atambo

- GW self‑energies exhibit causality violations; GW+DMFT, though incorporating exact local self‑energies, still fails to enforce causality.
- The two‑site dimer hides the violation due to symmetry, while a frustrated three‑site ring exposes explicit Matsubara‑causality violations in both GW and GW+DMFT.
- Exact Lehmann moments show that the high‑frequency tail requires non‑local irreducible vertex corrections; the local‑only truncation in GW+DMFT leaves non‑local RPA resonances uncorrected.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00324)

### Interior contacts in a narrow quantum Hall bar: a two-dimensional self-consistent screening calculation of the current distribution

**Authors:** A. Siddiki

- 2D self‑consistent Thomas‑Fermi‑Poisson calculation of density, filling factor, and current in a 3 µm GaAs/AlGaAs Hall bar with interior contacts.
- Three regimes for interior contacts across the ν = 2 plateau: galvanically connected (compressible bulk), floating (Hall potential of incompressible bulk), isolated (closed incompressible rings).
- Two‑terminal resistance between interior contacts rises sharply in floating/isolated regimes; isolation interval ends before the low‑field edge of the plateau and is set by the disappearance of the incompressible bulk.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00326)

### From Quantized Hall Plateaus to Topological Surfaces: Quantum Capacitance as a Unifying Probe

**Authors:** A. Siddiki

- Quantum capacitance directly probes the density of states of topological boundary modes, linking quantum Hall plateaus and Dirac surface states.
- Self‑consistent screening splits the bulk into compressible/incompressible regions, yet plateaus stay quantised.
- Local capacitance shows the bulk is field‑dependent and non‑uniform, challenging the assumption of a uniformly gapped bulk.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00336)

### Thermodynamic origins of multimode spinodal decomposition in multicomponent alloys

**Authors:** Pravan Omprakash

- Multicomponent alloys typically decompose in one pseudobinary mode because unlike elements mix, limiting unstable composition directions.
- Balanced repulsive triangles and attractive–repulsive pairs can produce two comparable downhill directions, yielding locally varied solid solutions instead of distinct blocks.
- A phase‑specific screen found Cu‑In‑Ir‑Ti as a candidate with nearly balanced modes, illustrating a design route for microstructures via spinodal decomposition.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00351)
