### Deformation-induced amorphous complexion transitions elevate strength and ductility

**Authors:** Masoud Ahmadi, Jin Qin, Gabrielle Tiph\'ene, Mohamed Charai, Alejandro G\'omez-P\'erez, Khalid Hoummada, Thomas Pardoen, Matteo Ghidelli, Hosni Idrissi

- Plastic deformation at room temperature creates amorphous intergranular films in CuZr nanocrystals, mainly at incoherent twin boundaries.
- These films show short‑ to medium‑range order gradients, forming metallic‑glass‑like cores that enhance strength and toughness.
- The transition doubles yield strength, fracture strain, and tensile toughness versus a reference alloy.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20908)

### Bootstrapping Disordered Quantum Systems

**Authors:** Yaprak \"Onder, Michael G. Scheer, Minjae Cho, Eslam Khalaf

- Bootstrap framework bounds disorder‑averaged ground‑state observables by extending operator algebra to include disorder variables and encoding distributions via moments.
- Applied to 1D random transverse‑field Ising model, producing tight bounds on energy density, short‑range spin‑spin correlators, and energy variance for discrete, uniform, and Gaussian disorder.
- The bootstrap gap (upper‑minus‑lower bound) maps the phase diagram, remaining small in paramagnetic/ferromagnetic phases and growing in the Griffiths regime.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20916)

### Metallic N\'eel order stabilized by coupling between inequivalent Hubbard layers

**Authors:** Yi-Ming Wu, Jessica Yiqi Pan, Hong Yao, Steven A. Kivelson

- Two inequivalent Hubbard layers analyzed with unrestricted Hartree‑Fock.
- Strong interlayer coupling suppresses stripe order, yielding a commensurate Néel‑ordered metal.
- The metallic phase displays hole pockets at (±π/2, ±π/, matching ARPES data for multilayer cuprates.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20922)

### Bootstrapping Classical Systems with Quenched Disorder

**Authors:** Minjae Cho, Yaprak \"Onder, Eslam Khalaf, Michael G. Scheer

- Bootstrap framework for disorder‑averaged observables in classical systems using positivity, equations of motion, and explicit coupling expectations.
- Constraints cast as linear and semidefinite programs, yielding bounds on single‑ and two‑replica correlators via Gram‑matrix positivity.
- Applied to 2D random‑site Ising and 1D disordered contact process; identifies Griffiths‑region kinks and recovers known correlation‑length exponent.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20927)

### Smectic Superconductivity

**Authors:** Grayson R. Frazier, Erez Berg

- Soft nonunitary magnetic fluctuations in a 2D Rashba superconductor act as an emergent gauge field, renormalizing superfluid stiffness.
- The system enters a smectic superconducting state with a finite‑momentum helical phase and vanishing stiffness in one direction, destroying quasi‑long‑range order at any finite temperature.
- Weak lattice anisotropy pins the ferromagnetic order, restoring finite stiffness and enabling a vortex‑deconfined regime near magnetic onset, relevant for graphene/TMD heterostructures.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20932)

### Pressure and Proximity Tuned Twisted Bilayer Graphene

**Authors:** David T. S. Perkins, Joseph J. Betouras

- Encapsulated twisted bilayer graphene exhibits non‑radial spin texture yet maintains a purely collinear Edelstein effect.
- Uniaxial pressure tunes the band structure, changing the number of active Fermi surfaces without disrupting the collinear response.
- Higher pressure spreads Berry curvature over large regions of the moiré Brillouin zone, altering the system’s quantum geometry and topology.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20958)

### Shape Deformation and Braid Statistics of Fractional Quantum Hall Quasiparticles

**Authors:** Mytraya Gattu, J. K. Jain

- Berry phase of a FQH quasiparticle loop decomposes into AB, shape‑dependent, and braid parts.
- Shape term can exceed the order‑one braid contribution, obscuring precise braid statistics.
- Interference phase jumps can be explained without invoking sharply quantized edge quasiparticle charges.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20985)

### Faulted loop nucleation and dopant activation in Al-implanted 4H-SiC

**Authors:** Sabine Leroch, Robert Stella, Andreas H\"ossinger, Lado Filipovic

- High‑temperature (≈900 K) Al implantation forms interstitial‑rich planar clusters that evolve into faulted interstitial loops during annealing, trapping Al and reducing substitutional activation.
- Lower implantation temperatures preserve disorder that is consumed during regrowth, leading to higher chemical activation within MD‑accessible annealing times.
- Faulted loops nucleate via merging of locally faulted regions; activation energies are ~1 eV for dislocation nucleation and ~1 eV for stacking‑fault growth, with Frank loops dominating at high T and Shockley partials at early stages

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20986)

### Singular high-harmonic transport above a quantum threshold

**Authors:** Yugo Onishi, Su-Yang Xu, Liang Fu

- Landau‑Zener tunneling in small‑gap insulators produces a singular DC I‑V relation and strong high‑harmonic generation.
- Harmonic amplitudes decay subexponentially with order, slower than in ordinary conductors or PN diodes.
- A scaling law links the nth‑harmonic current amplitude to the applied field, enabling THz generation via solid‑state frequency multiplication.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20990)

### Dual-Sublattice Ferromagnetism Driven by Cooperative Double Exchange and Superexchange at NdNiO$_3$/CaMnO$_3$ Interfaces

**Authors:** Sharup Sheikh, Uditha M. Jayathilake, Michael Terilli, Mikhail Kareev, Jay R. Paudel, Arian Arab, Christoph Klewe, Tien-Lin Lee, Jak Chakhalian, Alexander X. Gray

- Interfacial valence reconstruction produces Mn³⁺ on CaMnO₃ and Ni²⁺ on NdNiO₃ sides.
- Mn⁴⁺–O–Mn³⁺ double exchange in CaMnO₃ and Ni²⁺–O–Mn⁴⁺ superexchange across the interface generate dual‑sublattice ferromagnetism.
- XMCD shows ferromagnetic moments on both Mn and Ni, with Ni magnetism concentrated in the interfacial Ni²⁺ component.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21002)

### Resonant spin mode from retarded relaxation

**Authors:** J. Beater, G. S. Uhrig

- THz spin resonances in ultrafast dynamics exceed conventional Landau‑Lifshitz predictions; inertial LL‑Gilbert only partially fixes this.
- Retarded Lindbladian relaxation explains the multiple, nearly equidistant peaks with realistic parameters matching positions and widths.
- The phenomenological model reproduces the large resonance widths and spacing observed experimentally.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21623)

### Reformulating Pfaffian Quantum Monte Carlo with the Hybrid Monte Carlo formalism

**Authors:** Thomas Hauschild, Lin Wang, Finn L. Temmen, Thomas Luu

- Pfaffian QMC combined with Hybrid Monte Carlo achieves \(O(N_\tau L^\) scaling, matching DQMC but without pseudofermions.
- The MD force depends only on the Pfaffian magnitude, allowing a determinant representation and cheap unequal‑time Majorana correlators.
- Tested on the interacting Kitaev chain, it reproduces exact diagonalization and resolves boundary‑mode overlap across the topological transition.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21631)

### Orbital character and photoemission signatures of the A-pocket in infinite-layer nickelates

**Authors:** Leonard M. Verhoff, Michael Seidl, Liang Si, Karsten Held

- A‑pocket in NdNiO₂ is a strongly hybridized state comprising interstitial‑s, Nd‑d_xy, and Ni‑d_xz/yz orbitals.
- ARPES simulations confirm that interstitial‑s matrix elements are essential to reproduce the measured Fermi‑surface polarization; omitting them yields disagreement.
- Proper spectral assignment in infinite‑layer nickelates demands simultaneous consideration of orbital hybridization and photoemission matrix elements.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21665)

### Machine learning magnetic interactions from neutron powder diffraction data

**Authors:** Adit S. Desai, Yongqiang Cheng, Joseph A. M. Paddison

- ML predicts isotropic interaction parameters from powder neutron diffuse scattering with ~2 % accuracy on eight high‑symmetry lattices.
- It avoids the false‑minimum problem of conventional non‑linear least‑squares refinement.
- Powder diffuse scattering thus provides a compact fingerprint of a material’s magnetic Hamiltonian.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21970)

### Unified Field Bosonization Technique for strongly inhomogenous Luttinger Liquids

**Authors:** Soundarya P, Venkata Suryanarayana M, Joy Prakash Das

- Unified Field Bosonization Technique (UFBT) delivers closed‑form N‑point correlators for 1D Luttinger liquids with static impurities, bypassing RG or perturbation theory.
- Validation shows known limits, first‑order interaction agreement, and Schwinger‑Dyson consistency; correlation exponents stay impurity‑independent, only amplitudes and spatial profiles change.
- Enables analytic studies of transport, Friedel oscillations, and local/dynamic density of states in strongly inhomogeneous Luttinger liquids.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21973)

### Correlation enhanced magnetism at a superconducting cuprate/manganite interface

**Authors:** Vitor A. M. Lima, Marcello Civelli, Walber Hugo de Brito

- Superconductivity coexists with Cu spin polarization induced by a manganite interface.
- Correlations amplify magnetism, making the superconducting state carry a larger magnetic moment than the normal state.
- At high exchange fields, a first‑order transition destroys superconductivity, leaving a strongly spin‑polarized pseudogap.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22051)
