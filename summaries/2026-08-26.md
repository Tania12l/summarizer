### Electrostatic lens of a charged vortex

**Authors:** Iogann Tolbatov, Luca Salasnich

- Derived the near‑axis electrostatic potential of a charged vortex with radially varying density and compressibility.
- Expressed the constant, quadratic, and quartic coefficients in terms of local screening and vortex‑density gradients, including a thermodynamic term from compressibility.
- Applied to a 3D unitary Fermi gas, separating equation‑of‑state contributions from ordinary screening and charge‑source terms, linking vortex‑core thermodynamics to the electrostatic profile.

### Dynamical Signatures and Kibble-Zurek Scaling of Localization in Tilted Bose-Einstein Condensates

**Authors:** Argha Debnath, Radheshyam Saha, Ravindra W. Chhajlany, Mariusz Gajda, Debraj Rakshit

- Tilt strength controls localization–delocalization crossover in a 1D BEC; repulsive interactions promote delocalization.
- Sudden quenches show localized condensates with high survival probability, revivals, and narrow spectra, while delocalized ones exhibit weak recurrences, irregular dynamics, and broad spectra.
- Finite‑rate ramps across the threshold reveal Kibble‑Zurek scaling of the localization length and Bogoliubov gap, yielding critical exponents.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21492)

### Strain-driven spin-flop transition and collapse of the giant magnon gap in the bilayer iridate Sr$_3$Ir$_2$O$_7$

**Authors:** Choong H. Kim

- Biaxial compression (~–4 %) flips Sr₃Ir₂O₇’s easy axis from the c‑axis to the ab‑plane, collapsing its giant magnon gap.
- A first‑principles magnetic Hamiltonian (no fitting) shows interlayer exchange suppression and in‑plane exchange strengthening drive the flop; Hund’s coupling > 15 eliminates the collinear state.
- Compressed films sit on a metamagnetic boundary ending at a zero‑temperature bicritical point, enabling charge‑neutral control of moment direction.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22473)

### Anomalous dynamical energy flows via nonlinear phononics in spin-Peierls chains

- Short pulses keep the response perturbative, preserving sharp Fano‑like interferences and enabling magnetic control without quenching lattice modes.
