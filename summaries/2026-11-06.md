### Where heat flows and where to change it: gauge-free sensitivity maps of thermal conductivity in disordered solids

**Authors:** D. A. Drabold, C. Ugwumadu

- Developed two local conductivity maps in harmonic theory: a gauge‑dependent atom‑projection and a gauge‑free mass/bond‑stiffness response obeying an Euler sum rule.
- The two maps are anti‑correlated: heavy atoms reduce κ where heat flow is low, but increase κ where flow is high, explained by local softness and mode mixing.
- Temperature dependence shows the anti‑correlation vanishes at room temperature (fixed vibrational amplitude) but persists below ~100 K; paracrystalline silicon grains conduct more heat per atom.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06958)

### Stability and Relaxation of Electron-Phonon Systems at Strong Coupling

**Authors:** Noga Bashan, Erez Berg

- Non‑Markovian Kadanoff‑Baym dynamics eliminates the instability of Migdal‑Eliashberg theory at strong coupling.
- In the intermediate temperature window (Debye < T < coupling), the relaxation rate is linear in T with a Planckian slope and shows strongly oscillatory decay.
- Any pre‑superconducting instability must arise beyond MET (e.g., phonon softening, polaronic effects), so the phonon frequency is not a fundamental Tc bound.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07014)

**Authors:** Giuseppe D'Auria, Alessandro Silva

- Edwards–Anderson order overshoots for low‑energy shells and undershoots for high‑energy shells, driven by eigenstate rigidity versus pair‑pattern spread.

### Extensions of the Haldane gap proof: higher dimensions, near degeneracy, fermions and tensor-network states

**Authors:** Tarun Grover

- Extends Haldane‑gap proof to arbitrary dimensions using space‑time duality, bounding splittings of near‑degenerate low‑energy states while proving a nonzero gap.
- Applies to 2+1‑D toric code and transverse‑field Ising model, showing splittings ≤ e⁻ᶜL (or e⁻ᶜL²) and a finite gap in the thermodynamic limit.
- Generalizes to fermionic systems (symmetric mass generation) and tensor‑network states, establishing exponential decay of all local correlations.

- Time‑of‑flight neutron spectroscopy mapped well‑defined magnon dispersions in NiBr₂.
