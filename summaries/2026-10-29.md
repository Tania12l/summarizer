### Quantum-magneto oscillations in a supramolecular Mn(II)-[3 x 3] grid

**Authors:** O. Waldmann, S. Carretta, P. Santini, R. Koch, A. G. M. Jansen, G. Amoretti, R. Caciuffo, L. Zhao, L. K. Thompson

### Ferromagnetism in Fe-doped SnO2 thin films

**Authors:** J. M. D. Coey, A. P. Douvalis, C. B. Fitzgerald, M. Venkatesan

- Fe‑doped SnO₂ thin films are transparent ferromagnets with TC ≈ 610 K and Ms ≈ 2 Am²/kg.
- Mossbauer spectra reveal high‑spin Fe³⁺; only ~23 % of Fe ions are magnetically ordered, each carrying ~8 µB.
- Ferromagnetism attributed to Fe³⁺ coupling through an electron trapped in an oxygen vacancy (F‑center).

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0401293v1)

**Authors:** V. K. Thorsmolle, R. D. Averitt, X. Chi, D. J. Hilton, D. L. Smith, A. P. Ramirez, A. J. Taylor

**Authors:** A. D. Jackson, G. M. Kavoulakis, E. Lundh

- Derived a universal phase diagram; continuous transitions exact for weak coupling and small anharmonicity.
- Variational predictions agree with Gross‑Pitaevskii numerical solutions.

### Observation of narrow fluorescence from doubly driven four-level atoms at room temperature

**Authors:** Uday Kumar Khan, Jimmy Sebastian, N. Kamaraju, Andal Narayanan, R. Srinivasan, Hema Ramachandran

- Narrow fluorescence peaks appear in Rb‑85 under dual‑laser 3D molasses; one laser fixed detuned from cooling transition, the other scanned over repumping lines.
- Peaks form symmetric pairs whose separation increases with cooling‑laser detuning; large detunings yield additional small peaks.

### Origin of asymmetric reversal modes in ferromagnetic/antiferromagnetic multilayers

**Authors:** B. Beckmann, U. Nowak, K. D. Usadel

- Asymmetric reversal modes appear in certain exchange‑bias multilayers.
- Domain‑state simulations show the effect depends on the angle between the AFM easy axis and the applied field.
- At specific angles the FM reverses symmetrically via coherent rotation; at others it reverses asymmetrically, sometimes producing zero perpendicular magnetization.

### Vortex waves and the onset of turbulence in $^3$He-B

**Authors:** K. L. Henderson, C. F. Barenghi

- Finne et al. identified the intrinsic turbulence onset criterion \(q=\alpha/(1-\alpha')<1\) in \(^{3}\)He‑B.
- The criterion corresponds to marginally damped Kelvin waves; for \(q>1\) waves cannot grow to trigger reconnections.
- Analysis of rotating‑superfluid modes confirms \(q=1\) as the crossover between damped and propagating Kelvin waves in the long‑wavelength limit.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0401212v1)

### New Developments in Diagrammatic Monte Carlo for Interacting Lattice Fermions: Efficient Algorithms and Applications

**Authors:** Boyuan Shi

- Developed inverse‑flavour expansion and CoS++/determinant‑polynomial sieve to accelerate diagrammatic Monte Carlo for lattice fermions, achieving 24–25× speed‑ups at ninth order.
- Applied the methods to large‑scale Hubbard models (up to 2048 sites) and computed high‑order thermodynamic observables, including a tenth‑order connected‑determinant calculation on a 3×3 cluster.
- Introduced a fermionic replica path integral for the second Renyi entropy, yielding precise results (e.g., \(S_2/N_s=06695\) on a 50×50 lattice) with Monte Carlo uncertainties \(0\times10^{-5}\).

### Coulomb Crystallization in Free Electron Beams

**Authors:** Valerio Di Giulio, Rudolf Haindl, Armin Feist, Claus Ropers

- Transient Coulomb crystals form in freely propagating electron pulses up to nine electrons, revealed by energy‑correlation measurements.
- Leading/trailing electrons generate a self‑induced longitudinal potential that temporarily confines inner electrons into an ordered chain.
- A statistical‑mechanical model links crystallization to beam geometry and Coulomb‑to‑thermal energy ratio, predicting sustained ordering with weak transverse guiding and spontaneous bunching in continuous beams.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37352v1)

### Quantum Fisher Information Deficit as a Geometric Signature of Strong-to-Weak Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking

**Authors:** Zhang-Chi Yin, Wei Zheng

- SW‑SSB evades charged two‑point correlators yet appears as a persistent deficit in the maximal QFI under symmetry‑neutral pair rotations.
- A model‑independent inequality hierarchy equates this QFI deficit with fidelity and Rényi‑1 correlators, yielding asymptotic long‑range‑order diagnostics.
- For symmetry‑projected Gibbs states, the deficit is bounded below by the mean energy injected by the pair operation, remaining finite at any finite temperature due to locality, thus providing an information‑geometric signature of thermal SW‑SSB.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37092v1)

### Spin-polarized tunneling from a Kondo insulator: equilibrium surface magnetism versus magneto-electric response in SmB$_6$

**Authors:** Maxim Dzero, Brian M. Andersen

- Spin‑polarized tunneling from SmB₆ to Fe₁₊ₓTe requires surface time‑reversal symmetry breaking; contrast comes from equilibrium surface magnetism and a field‑induced magneto‑electric moment.

- The equilibrium/field‑induced ratio equals ±2 × bias at the sign‑change

### Robustness of Anyonic Time-Domain Interference Against Dephasing

**Authors:** Hai-Dong Li, Hao-Xiang Long, Gu Zhang, D. Y. Xing, Wei Chen

- TDB interference decays algebraically with dephasing, whereas spatial interference decays exponentially.
- Overlapping temporal paths cancel most environmental phase noise, leaving only a small nonoverlapping window to dephase.
- A Fabry‑Perot fractional quantum Hall device can directly compare TDB and spatial interference, demonstrating TDB’s robustness.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36686v1)

### Where Does the Anion-Redox Hole Go? Reliability of Open-Shell Projection-Based Embedding for Li-Rich Cathodes, from DFT Environments to Quantum Solvers

**Authors:** Pritam Das, Satadeep Bhattacharjee, Pil-Ryung Cha

- Developed spin‑unrestricted projection‑based embedding, achieving microhartree accuracy for closed‑, broken‑symmetry, and high‑spin DFT references.
- Applied DMRG‑in‑DFT to Li₂₋ₓMnO₃ clusters; the lowest SCF state consistently places the anion‑redox hole on an O₂ pair, with hole distribution varying by up to 5 e depending on exchange fraction and geometry.
- Across 82 runs, embedded mean‑field reproduces the hole location (median DMRG change 02–04 e); spin projection across the active/environment boundary can shift integer spin by up to 26 e, but including Mn neighbors reduces this to ≤09 e.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36659v1)
