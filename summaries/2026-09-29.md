### From a three-fluid junction to Young's law: Constitutive and rigid-support limits of wetting

**Authors:** Dieter Bothe

- Links Neumann balance at a three‑fluid junction to Young’s law through a viscoelastic continuum limit.
- Demonstrates that stiffening a solid suppresses displacement yet permits reversible wet‑dry migration, producing Young’s angle while retaining capillary reaction forces.
- Provides two rigorous convergence results (finite‑strain clamped model and quadratic half‑space model) explaining coexistence of local Neumann ridges with distant Young angles.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31672)

### Blockwise friction heterogeneity redistributes stretching in a 2D Gaussian polymer under Batchelor-Kraichnan random flow

**Authors:** Arpan Dey

- Friction heterogeneity leaves equilibrium conformation unchanged but changes block mobilities and relaxation spectrum.
- In a Batchelor‑Kraichnan flow, the high‑friction block stretches more than the low‑friction block, with asymmetry increasing with friction contrast.
- Random strain preferentially amplifies the slowest mode, and the effect persists even when global relaxation timescales are matched.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31707)

### R-transforms for non-Hermitian block random matrices: a spherical integral approach

**Authors:** Pierre Bousseyroux

- Extends spherical‑integral method to compute $\mathcal{R}$‑transforms for non‑Hermitian matrices with a fixed $K\times K$ block structure.
- Introduces a rank‑$K$ spherical integral yielding a scalar $H$‑transform on $2K\times2K$ overlap matrices and a matrix‑valued $\mathcal{R}$‑transform capturing joint block correlations.
- Uses replica analysis to conjecture a subordination relation for sums of independent block matrices, enabling determination of spectral boundaries for large block‑structured non‑Hermitian ensembles.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31730)

### Read-Rezayi fractional Chern insulators in modulated Bernal graphene

**Authors:** Ruth Mora-Soto, Andr\'e Grossi Fonseca, Raul Perea-Causin, Hui Liu, Emil J. Bergholtz, Marin Solja\v{c}i\'c

- Realized a $\mathbb{Z}_3$ Read‑Rezayi fractional Chern insulator at $\nu=3/5$ in modulated Bernal bilayer graphene with screened Coulomb interactions.
- Band structure emulates lowest and first Landau levels; at $\nu=1/2$ the ground state corresponds to the Moore‑Read state.
- Employed target‑phase optimization with band‑projected exact diagonalization to efficiently identify non‑Abelian phases in realistic high‑dimensional models.

### Mechanical Clustering of Cells via the ExtraCellular Matrix

**Authors:** Ran Glinowiecki, Shahar Goren, Oren Tchaicheeyan, Bar Ergaz, Robin L. B. Selinger, Yair Shokef, Ayelet Lesman

- Contractile cells form long‑range aligned/densified ECM fiber bands that mechanically couple cells into clusters.
- Finite‑element modeling shows ~10 % cell volume fraction suffices for internal stiffening via stress‑band mediation, far below percolation threshold.
- Percolation of these fiber bands triggers a sharp transition to bulk stiffening, illustrating long‑range cell–cell mechanical communication shapes tissue mechanics.

### Connecting finite-size scaling and renormalization-group flows in Anderson localization on random graphs

**Authors:** Ignacio Garc\'ia-Mata, Weitao Chen, John Martin, Bertrand Georgeot, Gabriel Lemari\'e

- Finite‑size scaling of eigenfunction moments and RG flows of multifractal dimensions $D_q$ are connected in the Anderson transition on small‑world random graphs.
- RG flows for $D_q$ with $q<1/2$ confirm a strongly multifractal localized phase, clearly distinct from the localized regime at strong disorder.
- A single beta function for eigenfunction moments collapses data across disorder strengths, supporting single‑parameter scaling and excluding Kosterlitz‑Thouless‑like two‑parameter behavior.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31872)

- CeGaGe transitions from achiral I4₁md to chiral P4₃ at low T, enabling a chiral Weyl‑Kondo semimetal.

### Kitaev-Heisenberg model on the square-hexagon-dodecagon lattice

**Authors:** Saeed Barari, Yasir Iqbal, Ganapathy Baskaran, Saeed S. Jahromi, Chunxiao Liu, Julien Vidal

- Gapped toric‑code phase in the anisotropic Kitaev model on the SHD lattice, with a single Dirac point at the phase boundary; vortex excitations on square, hexagon, and dodecagon plaquettes realize distinct \(e/m\) anyon assignments.
- In the isotropic Kitaev–Heisenberg model, robust toric‑code phases persist near the Kitaev limits, while four collinear magnetic orders emerge, identified via PEPS, ED, and spin‑wave calculations.
- The full phase diagram is mapped by tracking Majorana zero modes and vortex‑bound states, delineating transitions between topological and magnetic phases.

### Scaling Theory for Learning Low-Energy Quantum Subspaces

**Authors:** Guijing Duan, Chen Mo, Di Luo

- Low‑energy subspace can be learned efficiently from nearby sampled states; error scales as \(d^{2(q+}\).
- For gapped local ground states on \(n\) sites, error \(\sim d^2 (n d^^q\); \(nd^2\) is the natural scaling variable, giving sampling cost \(K=O([\log(1/\varepsilon)]^D)\).
- Numerical results on Heisenberg and transverse‑field Ising chains confirm the scaling law and enable finite‑size probes of criticality.
