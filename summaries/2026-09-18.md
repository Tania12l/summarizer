### Hysteresis in Atomic Josephson Junctions

**Authors:** Vijay Pal Singh, Ludwig Mathey, Luigi Amico

- Demonstrated hysteresis and retrapping in an underdamped atomic Josephson junction using a velocity‑sweep protocol.
- Classical‑field simulations of a 2‑D bosonic superfluid reproduce the hysteresis loop and retrapping current, matching the RCSJ model.
- Hysteresis originates from persistent phase slips and vortex‑antivortex nucleation; the retrapping current follows Stewart‑McCumber scaling, linking atomic and superconducting junctions.

### Bad metallicity in the semi-quantum regime of the Hubbard model

**Authors:** Evyatar Tulipman, Vadim Oganesyan, Thomas P. Devereaux, Steven A. Kivelson

- Infinite‑\(U\) Hubbard model at low hole density exhibits a semi‑quantum regime: \(T\)-linear resistivity above the Ioffe–Regel limit while compressibility remains \(T\)-independent.
- Cooling induces quasi‑local ferromagnetic “spin cages” around doped holes, enabling local quantum motion within a fluctuating spin background.
- The resulting bad‑metal behavior mirrors that seen in many materials, underscoring the generic influence of strong local correlations.

**Authors:** Yin Shi, Xuande Bu, Sheng Meng

- Three‑Q loop‑current order on the kagome lattice melts either directly or in two stages, leaving an intermediate phase with long‑range chirality but restored translational symmetry.
- The two‑stage transition occurs when chirality‑changing domain walls are only ~12–13 % more costly than same‑chirality translation walls.
- Ginzburg–Landau scaling shows the vestigial chiral order persists with amplitude fluctuations, providing a thermodynamic route to time‑reversal symmetry breaking above the CDW critical temperature.

### Fractional quantum spin Hall crystals from hidden density-wave order

**Authors:** Matthew Shammami, Sudip Chakravarty

- Mixed singlet‑triplet d‑density wave produces hidden bond order, breaking translation symmetry and yielding time‑reversed Chern bands without conventional charge or spin density.
- At odd‑denominator fractional fillings, interactions transform this symmetry‑broken topological state into a fractional quantum spin Hall liquid with crossed charge‑spin transport.
- Phase boundaries: strong inter‑spin coupling or dilute fillings favor charge order; at half filling, spin‑decoupled limit gives a homogeneous correlated liquid akin to a composite Fermi liquid.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19638)
