### A unified closed-form theory for elastic waves in polycrystals of any crystal symmetry

**Authors:** Ming Huang, Stanislav I. Rokhlin, Michael J. S. Lowe

- Closed‑form mass operator for coherent elastic waves in equiaxed, untextured polycrystals of any symmetry, yielding an explicit dispersion equation valid at all frequencies.
- Extends Stanke–Kino cubic‑grain results, includes Born and far‑field limits, and shows three coherent roots per polarization (incident + two scattering channels with/without mode conversion).
- Gives closed‑form limits for velocity, attenuation, and partition in Rayleigh, stochastic, and geometric regimes, explaining the transition from a single to multiple coherent waves with wavelength.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26674)

### Topologically Obstructed Kondo Hybridization: A Route to Kondo Semimetals

**Authors:** Yue Liu, Junze Deng, Haoyu Hu

- Topological invariants of the conduction‑electron Fermi surface can forbid a full Kondo hybridization gap, forcing hybridization zeros at specific momenta.
- Hybridization zeros produce c‑f band crossings, yielding nodal points or lines near the Fermi energy within the Kondo phase.
- This mechanism offers a route to Kondo semimetals, linking Fermi‑surface topology to emergent correlated phases.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26686)

### Off-diagonal long-range order and $p$-wave pairing in one dimension from critical density fluctuations

**Authors:** Matthew D. Thomson, Simon M. Martin, Frank Marsiglio, Joseph Maciejko

- Critical density fluctuations near phase‑separation act as pairing glue, overcoming disordering effects of phase fluctuations.

### Geometric Contribution to the Electron-Phonon Coupling Constant: Checkerboard and Hyper-diamond Models

**Authors:** Thomas Caligiure, Daniel P. Arovas, Jiabin Yu

- Quantum metric provides an independent, tunable contribution to electron‑phonon coupling (EPC) in the checkerboard lattice.
- In the checkerboard model, this geometric term can be enhanced while the energetic part remains fixed, boosting overall EPC.
- For the hyper‑diamond lattice, the geometric contribution accounts for ~50 % of the total EPC in any dimension, extending the graphene result to higher‑dimensional systems.

### Quantum order by disorder among lozenge tilings: a staircase of hub-free crystals between the Lieb ferrimagnet and the antiferromagnet

**Authors:** Suhwan Yoo, Jeehoon Choi, Chaeyeon Kim, Hee Young Kwon, Changyeon Won

- Quantum fluctuations lift the classical degeneracy of lozenge‑tiling networks, selecting square‑type tilings through a convex zero‑point energy that depends on site coordination.
- A magnetic field couples to the Lieb moment, favoring three‑ and five‑fold sites and suppressing six‑fold hubs, limiting hub‑free tilings to 3/5 of the dice‑lattice moment.
- The classical jump is replaced by a staircase of periodic crystals: the widest step is a hub‑free crystal saturating the bound, followed by progressively denser three‑/five‑fold defect crystals.

### Role of antisite disorder in dictating the Ising anisotropy and ferromagnetic ground state of NdGaSi

**Authors:** Anyesh Saraswati, Sourav Kanthal, Subhajit Nandy, Jhuma Sannigrahi, Matthias Gutmann, Nitesh Kumar

- Ga/Si antisite disorder stabilizes the centrosymmetric I4₁/amd structure, raising Nd³⁺ site symmetry to D₂d and forbidding m = ±2 Stevens operators.
- The resulting ~97 % pure ground‑state doublet yields a collinear ferromagnet with Ising anisotropy along c below T_C ≈ 11 K.
- This CEF purity selects a k = 0 ferromagnetic ground state, contrasting the incommensurate ferrimagnet of ordered NdAlSi, demonstrating disorder as a tuning knob for magnetic order.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27334)
