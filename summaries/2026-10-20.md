### Universal electrical resistivity in iron-based high-temperature superconductors

**Authors:** E. F. Talantsev

- Shows that IBS in‑plane resistivity follows a parallel‑channel model (Bloch‑Grüneisen + Arrhenius hopping).
- Attributes the varied ρ(T) shapes across 11, 111, 122, 1111, 1144, 42214 families to changes in Θ_D and E_a.
- Uses derived Θ_D to estimate electron‑phonon coupling λₑ₋ph, linking resistivity to superconducting pairing.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23865)

### Multiple propagating modes in random polycrystals

**Authors:** Ming Huang, Stanislav I. Rokhlin, Michael J. S. Lowe

- Finite‑element simulations show coherent elastic waves in isotropic polycrystals split into multiple propagating modes.
- When the wavelength nears the grain size, a single low‑frequency mode bifurcates into two observable modes for both transverse and longitudinal excitations, matching dispersion‑equation roots.
- The third theoretical root has much higher attenuation; attenuation hierarchy, not root count, dictates which modes actually propagate.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24047)

### Quantifying the Kinetic and Thermodynamic Origins of the Glass-Forming Ability in Metals

**Authors:** Weiwei Jin, Jinpeng Fan, Mark D. Shattuck, Corey S. O'Hern

- GFA measured by critical cooling rate \(R_c\), strongly correlated with crystallization rate \(\mathcal{K}_X\).
- \(\mathcal{K}_X=(IU^^{1/4}\) splits into kinetic (attachment/diffusion) and thermodynamic (nucleation barrier) terms; thermodynamics dominates.
- Nucleation barrier \(\Delta G_c \sim \tilde\gamma^3/\Delta\tilde\mu^2\); the balance of interfacial energy \(\tilde\gamma\) and driving force \(\Delta\tilde\mu\) varies across materials.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24101)

### Kohler's Rule Holds in the Strange Metallic Hubbard Model

**Authors:** Rebekah M. Jin, Emily Z. Zhang, Jixun K. Ding, Brian Moritz, Thomas P. Devereaux

- Kohler’s rule surprisingly holds in the hole‑doped Hubbard‑Hofstadter model despite T‑linear resistivity and lack of quasiparticles.
- The rule emerges below a crossover temperature \(T^\star \approx t\), marked by local singlet formation and changes in double occupancy and specific heat.
- Findings suggest that strong correlations and strange metallicity alone do not cause anomalous magnetoresistance; Kohler scaling reflects a broader many‑body quantum coherence.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24208)

### Role of chirality in the polarizer-filter dichotomy in nonmagnetic junctions

**Authors:** Sergei Urazhdin

- Spin polarizers need chiral junctions; achiral junctions act as spin filters.
- Junction symmetry—not just molecular chirality—determines polarizing behavior; geometry can toggle achiral molecules between polarizer and filter.
- Linear two‑terminal MR cannot detect polarization; a nonlocal measurement distinguishes polarizers from filters and probes time‑reversal symmetry breaking.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24226)

### Theory of High-Temperature Fractional Chern Phases in Monolayer van der Waals Kagome Materials A$_2$T$_3$X$_4$

**Authors:** Soomin Oh, Jiyong Han, Jinhyeok Choi, Donghae Seo, Mingu Kang, Gil Young Cho

- Monolayer A₂T₃X₄ kagome sheets provide intrinsic topological flat bands and strong Coulomb interactions, enabling high‑temperature fractional Chern insulators.
- Exact diagonalization reveals ferromagnetism over wide fillings, producing fractional Chern insulators, composite Fermi liquids, and charge‑ordered states; Cs₂Pd₃S₄ shows a ~26 meV many‑body gap.
- Bilayer stacks with antiparallel magnetizations support fractional quantum spin Hall insulators at realistic interlayer separations.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24633)

### Interaction-driven competing instabilities in two-dimensional checkerboard quadratic band crossing semimetals

**Authors:** Jia-Hao Wen, Wen-Hao Bian, Jing Wang

- Analyzed all 16 marginal short‑range interactions in a 2D checkerboard QBCP semimetal using momentum‑shell RG, uncovering single, discrete, and continuous fixed‑point families.
- Determined dominant instabilities—nematic spin‑nematic, quantum anomalous Hall, chiral superconductivity, and quantum spin Hall—whose hierarchy depends on RG trajectories.
- Showed off‑diagonal interactions generate new orders, continuous fixed‑point groups allow systematic tuning of the leading phase, and full RG flow, not just fixed points, dictates the final state.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25046)

### Large exchange bias from coupled antiferromagnetic order and glassy magnetic freezing in $Co_{0.36}NbS_{2}$

**Authors:** Suman Mondal (Department of Physics, Ben-Gurion University of the Negev, Beer-Sheva 84105, Israel), Himadri Roy Dakua (Department of Physics, Ben-Gurion University of the Negev, Beer-Sheva 84105, Israel), Raz Luger (Department of Physics, Ben-Gurion University of the Negev, Beer-Sheva 84105, Israel), Muntaser Naamneh (Department of Physics, Ben-Gurion University of the Negev, Beer-Sheva 84105, Israel), Eran Maniv (Department of Physics, Ben-Gurion University of the Negev, Beer-Sheva 84105, Israel)

- Co₀.₃₆NbS₂ orders antiferromagnetically at TN ≈ 26 K and exhibits glassy freezing of a subset of moments below TB ≈ 12 K.
- Field‑cooling produces a large exchange‑bias shift (|HEB| ≈ 4 kOe at 8 K) that disappears above TB.
- The bias shows training, sweep‑field, and cooling‑field dependence, indicating coupling of frozen uncompensated moments to the antiferromagnetic matrix.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25102)

### Frustration-Assisted Electrical Switching in $Co_xTaS_2$

**Authors:** Matan Sterenberg (Department of Physics, Ben-Gurion University of the Negev, Beer-Sheva 84105, Israel), Bar Segura (Department of Physics, Ben-Gurion University of the Negev, Beer-Sheva 84105, Israel), Muntaser Naamneh (Department of Physics, Ben-Gurion University of the Negev, Beer-Sheva 84105, Israel), Eran Maniv (Department of Physics, Ben-Gurion University of the Negev, Beer-Sheva 84105, Israel)

- Demonstrated nonvolatile anisotropic resistance switching in near‑stoichiometric CoₓTaS₂ driven by current pulses, with activation current density ~10× lower than Hall‑active phase.
- Identified two distinct temperature‑dependent dynamical regimes and slow, history‑dependent magnetic relaxation linked to frustration‑induced glassy dynamics.
- Established magnetic frustration and collective glassy behavior as key mechanisms enabling efficient electrical reconfiguration in this van der Waals antiferromagnet.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25112)
