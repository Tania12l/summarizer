### Fermi-Level Metal-d Character of Group-6 Bis-Hexahapto Bilayer Graphene

**Authors:** Mingguang Chen

- Group‑6 (Cr, Mo, W) bis‑hexahapto bilayer graphene is metallic, but Fermi‑level states are ~70 % metal d rather than carbon p.
- Compared to ionic CaC₆/C₁₂Ca and C₁₂Li controls, the group‑6 systems retain only ~15–23 % interstitial spectral weight at E_F, indicating reduced carbon‑derived interlayer character.
- η₆–η₆ coordination forces AA stacking, overturning Bernal preference by 400–600 meV, and the ordered phases are metastable versus bulk metal yet bound against decomposition.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00083)

### Green-function Zeros Encode Competing Mott and Charge-ordering Scales

**Authors:** Peizhi Mai, Philip W. Phillips

- Green‑function zeros encode ordering physics in strongly correlated insulators.
- Their dispersion, set by spin correlations and defect kinematics, jumps discontinuously at the Mott–checkerboard transition.
- The effect survives further‑neighbor hopping, underscoring zero dispersion as a robust probe of competing Mott and charge‑ordering scales.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00124)

### Bayesian Tracking of a Diffusing Target in Two and Three Dimensions

**Authors:** Ewan McCulloch, Adam Nahum

- Maps Bayesian tracking of a diffusing target to KPZ growth/directed polymer, uncovering phase structure in 2D and 3D.
- In 2D, Bayes‑optimal inference always succeeds; suboptimal overconfidence can trigger a depinning failure transition.
- In 3D, three phases appear—success, delocalized failure, and localized‑but‑wrong failure—meeting at a Nishimori‑like multicritical point, with misspecification shifting the transitions.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00144)

**Authors:** Sam Oaks-Leaf, David T. Limmer

### Engineering tunable $p$-wave magnetism in antiferromagnetic bilayers

**Authors:** Yu-Han Lin, Jin-Wei Dong, Ziqiang Wang, Sen Zhou

- In-plane shift of two AB‑stacked AFM square lattices breaks spin‑degeneracy symmetries, producing odd‑parity \(p\)-wave spin splitting without spin‑orbit coupling.
- The splitting’s magnitude and direction, and the resulting spin responses, are continuously tunable by the relative displacement of the layers.
- Mean‑field Hubbard analysis reveals extended regions of spontaneous \(p\)-wave magnetism, including coplanar and chiral spin textures, establishing a minimal 2D platform for tunable \(p\)-wave magnetism.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00277)
