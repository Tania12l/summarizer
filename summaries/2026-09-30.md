### Memory-induced optimal switching in a dynamic Ising-Kuramoto model

**Authors:** Reon Hayashi

- Combines Ising spins and continuous phases in a globally coupled stochastic system with Glauber switching and Ornstein‑Uhlenbeck noise.
- Shows a nonmonotonic synchronization threshold \(K_c\) minimized at an optimal finite switching rate, balancing Glauber response against memory susceptibility.
- Confirms via theory and simulations the appearance of antiphase composite order, mode mixing, and a τ‑dependent shift of the optimal switching rate.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35838)

### Discrete Scale Invariance of Ising Mesons

**Authors:** Denis V. Vasilyev, Abbas Ali Saberi

- Discrete scale invariance emerges in mesons of a marginal \(1/r^4\) transverse‑field Ising chain via an inverse‑square attraction between dressed domain walls.
- The scale‑anomaly exponent, fixed by spontaneous magnetization and two‑kink threshold curvature, governs meson hierarchy, binding‑energy ratios, and log‑periodic scattering.
- Exact diagonalization and weak‑field analysis confirm coupled energy–size scaling, realizing quantum limit‑cycle physics in a many‑body magnetic system.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35940)

**Authors:** Zheng Zhou, Yin-Chen He

### Topological Band Theory for High-Dimensional Parameter Spaces

**Authors:** Vivek Chakrabhavi, Walt Chavarria, Jonathan J. Heckman, Steven Rayan

- Extends quantum‑Hall band theory to high‑dimensional flux‑parameter manifolds from genus‑>1 Riemann surfaces and higher‑form gauge couplings.
- Identifies richer topological invariants (first Chern class + higher curvature terms) that yield protected Kubo‑like responses from current correlations.
- Illustrates the framework with hyperbolic band theory on the genus‑two Bolza surface, relevant to recent synthetic‑dimension experiments.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35977)

### Nonreciprocal Chiral Automata

**Authors:** Andrew A. Allocca, Armin Rahmani, Pouyan Ghaemi, Sriram Ganeshan

- Introduces a lattice‑gas cellular automaton with chiral collisions that yields a nonzero odd pressure coefficient.
- Shows that a local parity‑breaking collision acts as an effective magnetic field, converting bulk viscosity into odd pressure.
- Derives general relations between Hall viscosity, odd pressure, and odd torque and their parity‑even counterparts.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35984)

### Linking Topological Medium Range Order and Density-Wave Coherence to Metallic Glass Ductility

**Authors:** Minhazul Islam, Muchen Wang, Andrea Fantin, Geun Hee Yoo, Ji Young Kim, Hamdan Ashfaq, Eun Soo Park, Robert Maass, Yunzhi Wang, Yue Fan, Hee-Suk Chung, Sang-Chul Lee, Jinwoo Hwang

- Combined synchrotron G(r) and 4D‑STEM to quantify geometric and topological medium‑range order in Zr‑based metallic glasses.
- G(r) coherence length predicts baseline ductility, while topological motifs (Cu‑rich FCC vs Zr‑rich HCP) modulate mechanical response.
- Increasing Zr suppresses FCC‑like MRO, enlarges coherence volume, and promotes HCP‑like order, enhancing ductility.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36041)

### Excitonic Stripe Order in the Two-Orbital Hubbard-Kanamori Model

**Authors:** Rafael D. Soares, Luke Staszewski, Chunhan Feng, Alexander Wietek

- Excitonic stripe order emerges as a spatially modulated inter‑orbital particle‑hole coherence in the two‑orbital Hubbard‑Kanamori model.
- The sign of Hund’s exchange selects the condensate: ferromagnetic Hund favors triplet excitons, antiferromagnetic Hund stabilizes singlet excitons.
- Doping produces incommensurate excitonic density waves (stripes and spiral triplet textures), while crystal‑field splitting reshapes nesting and can stabilize triplet order even without pair hopping.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36051)

### Measuring trainable degrees of freedom in materials graph neural networks: a random-subspace intrinsic dimension analysis

**Authors:** Shehroz Ahmad Shoaib, Kangming Li

- Uses random‑subspace intrinsic‑dimension analysis to measure how many trainable directions GNNs need to reach near‑final accuracy.
- Reveals task‑specific dimensional demands: metallic classification and bulk‑modulus need few subspace dimensions, while band‑gap, formation‑energy, and phonon predictions require more.
- Shows that larger model widths raise absolute dimensional thresholds but keep fractional thresholds stable, and that dataset size mainly impacts band‑gap models.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36084)

### Magnetic properties and Fermi-surface topology of kagome magnet HoV$_6$Sn$_6$

**Authors:** Tucker Beekmann, Ganesh Pokharel, Wyatt Edward Ackerman, Stephen D. Wilson, David E. Graf, Keshav Shrestha

- HoV₆Sn₆ orders ferromagnetically below ~4 K with strong anisotropy (≈5 μB /Ho along c, ≈7 μB /Ho in‑plane).
- Tunnel‑diode‑oscillator measurements reveal quantum oscillations up to ~9 kT, mapping a multi‑sheet Fermi surface and cyclotron masses.
- Density‑functional calculations show V‑kagome‑derived flat bands, van Hove singularities, Dirac crossings, and a V‑dominated DOS supporting a ferromagnetic ground state.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36258)

### Quantum metric induced nonlinear transport in the hidden loop-current phase of kagome metal RbV$_3$Sb$_5$

**Authors:** Jia-Chen Shi, Shupeng Xu, Utkarsh Khandelwal, Nashra Pistawala, Luminita Harnagea, Steven J. May, Ritesh Agarwal

- Third‑order nonlinear transport shows a sharp kink and strong anisotropy in the longitudinal cubic response at ~35 K, with a magnetic‑field‑odd transverse component, signaling a hidden low‑T phase with loop‑current order.
- The quantum‑metric quadrupole directly contributes to the longitudinal third‑order response, revealing time‑reversal symmetry breaking and electronic reconstruction.
- A loop‑current CDW model reproduces the observed angular dependence, demonstrating that higher‑order nonlinear transport can probe subtle symmetry changes via quantum‑metric effects.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36353)

- The μ⁺ suppresses nearest‑neighbor ¹⁴¹Pr spin dynamics, indicating a local alteration of the f‑electron state.

### Ergodicity in a hole-dopped Anderson spin insulator

**Authors:** M. S. Bahovadinov, A. A. Markov, G. V. Shlyapnikov

- A single hole in a 1D t‑JXX chain with random magnetic field can thermalize the spin sector at weak disorder, yielding ergodicity and hole delocalization.
- At stronger disorder the hole localizes with a finite length, restoring the Anderson spin insulator.
- Numerical results show a disorder‑controlled transition between these regimes.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36936)
